Who the exam is written for

The minimally qualified candidate (MQC) is a professional (operations, marketing, project management, education, communications, consulting) who uses Claude as a core productivity tool and builds Claude Projects. The guide places this person between casual prompt users and technical AI practitioners. They translate business goals into effective AI interactions, choose the right features, write structured prompts, critically evaluate output, adapt it for audiences, and know when human expertise, validation, or escalation is needed.

Out of scope: building against the API, designing agentic systems, enterprise-scale architecture, integrations, ML engineering. On the exam, those situations call for escalation to Claude Developers or Architects.

How questions are written

The official sample items are short workplace scenarios that ask for the most appropriate action or the choice that best fits the task. All four options often sound reasonable; you pick the best one. The three official samples test: verifying a confidently cited regulation subsection before sharing it, choosing a faster lower-cost model for high-volume simple replies, and anonymising personal identifiers before uploading data that policy restricts.

Wrong-answer patterns to recognise
  • Trusting confidence: “send it because Claude sounded sure” or “ask Claude to rate its own confidence”. Self-reported confidence is not a reliable accuracy signal.
  • Cosmetic fixes: reformatting or making text sound more formal does not fix accuracy.
  • Overkill: always using the most capable, most expensive model, or uploading everything “just in case”.
  • Giving up: skipping the task, disabling features, or switching platforms when a safe way to proceed exists.
  • Fake controls: telling Claude “don’t retain this” or “forget this” is not a data-protection control.
  • Ignoring policy: “it’s internal, so it’s fine” when organisational policy says otherwise.

AI Fluency and how Claude works

Foundation vocabulary used across all domains (AI Fluency, Claude 101, AI Capabilities & Limitations)

The 4D Framework

Anthropic’s AI Fluency course (built with Prof. Rick Dakan and Prof. Joseph Feller) defines AI fluency as working with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely through four competencies:

CompetencyCore questionSub-parts
DelegationShould AI do this, and which part?Problem awareness (understand your goal), platform awareness (know what the AI can and can’t do), task delegation (split work between human and AI)
DescriptionHow do I tell the AI what I need?Product description (what output), process description (how to approach it), performance description (how the AI should behave)
DiscernmentIs what came back good enough?Product discernment (the output), process discernment (the reasoning and approach), performance discernment (the AI’s behaviour)
DiligenceAm I using it responsibly, and do I own the result?Creation diligence (choose tools and data responsibly), transparency diligence (be honest about AI’s role), deployment diligence (take responsibility for what you share)

Two loops connect them. The Description–Discernment loop is the inner loop of describing, evaluating, and refining. The Delegation–Diligence loop is the outer loop of deciding what AI does and taking responsibility for it.

Three modes of human–AI interaction

  • Automation: AI performs a specific task based on your instructions (e.g., reformat 200 entries).
  • Augmentation: you and AI collaborate as thinking partners, iterating together (e.g., co-developing a strategy).
  • Agency: you configure AI to act independently, possibly for other people (e.g., a configured assistant that answers staff questions).

How a large language model works (enough for the exam)

  • Claude is a large language model (LLM) trained on large amounts of text. It generates responses by predicting likely next pieces of text (tokens). It is not looking facts up in a database unless it is given sources (uploaded files, web search, connectors).
  • Because it produces plausible text, it can produce plausible but false details: a hallucination.
  • Its built-in knowledge stops at a training cutoff. Recent information needs web search, Research, or your documents.
  • Everything Claude can “see” in a conversation sits inside a finite context window (your messages, its replies, files, instructions). Very long conversations use up the window and your usage limits faster.
  • Output is non-deterministic: the same prompt can produce different wording. Getting the same answer twice does not prove it is correct.
  • Models can be sycophantic: inclined to agree with the user’s framing. Ask for counter-arguments and evidence on both sides.

Capabilities vs limitations

Strong atWatch out for
Drafting, rewriting, summarising, translating, adapting toneInvented specifics: numbers, citations, quotes, URLs, section numbers
Analysing documents and data you provideMissing or outdated information past the training cutoff
Brainstorming, structuring, outlining, planningBias inherited from training data
Explaining concepts at different levelsLosing earlier details in very long conversations
Code, spreadsheets, files (with code execution)Overconfidence: fluent wording is not evidence of truth
🇺🇦 Простими словами

4D — це чотири навички: Delegation — вирішити, що віддати ШІ, а що зробити самій; Description — чітко пояснити задачу; Discernment — критично перевірити результат; Diligence — відповідальність: прозорість щодо використання ШІ і відповідальність за те, що ти відправляєш людям.

Claude не «знає» факти як база даних, він генерує найімовірніший текст. Тому він може впевнено вигадати цифру чи посилання. Впевнений тон ≠ правда.

Domain 1. Prompting and Task Execution

14% of the exam · about 8 questions

Official objectives

  • Create effective prompts for business and technical tasks
  • Apply task decomposition techniques to structure complex requests
  • Iterate prompts to improve output quality
  • Adapt prompting strategies by task type: analysis, research, drafting, brainstorming

Anatomy of an effective prompt

ElementWhat it doesExample
RoleSets perspective, vocabulary, expertise“You are an operations analyst at a logistics company.”
ContextBackground Claude can’t guess: audience, purpose, situation“This goes to regional managers who weren’t at the meeting.”
TaskOne clear instruction with an action verb“Summarise the three decisions and their owners.”
ConstraintsLength, tone, scope, what to include or avoid“Under 150 words, neutral tone, no internal jargon.”
Output formatStructure of the answer“A table: decision, owner, deadline.”
ExamplesShow what good looks like (few-shot / multishot prompting)Paste one past summary you liked.
Success criteriaHow you will judge the result“Every decision must name an owner; mark gaps as [missing].”

Techniques Anthropic recommends

  • Be clear and direct. Treat Claude like a smart new colleague with no context: explain the goal and why.
  • Tell Claude what to do, not only what not to do (“write in flowing paragraphs” works better than “no bullet points”).
  • Use examples to lock in format and style.
  • Separate parts of the prompt clearly, e.g. with headings or XML tags like <document> … </document>, so instructions and source material don’t blur together.
  • Ask Claude to think step by step for complex reasoning (or use extended thinking).
  • Give permission to say “I don’t know” or to mark missing information instead of guessing.
  • Ground in sources: “Answer only from the attached documents and quote the passage you used.”

Task decomposition

Task decomposition means splitting a complex request into smaller steps you can check. When each step’s output feeds the next prompt, that’s prompt chaining. Example: (1) extract facts from transcripts → (2) you check the facts → (3) draft a summary from the checked facts → (4) adapt the summary for executives. Benefits: each step is easier for Claude, errors are caught early, and you can reuse steps.

Iteration

The first output is a draft. Improve it by giving specific feedback (“shorten section 2 to three sentences; the audience is finance, so lead with cost impact”) rather than regenerating the same prompt and hoping. Change one thing at a time so you know what helped. When a fix works, carry it back into your reusable prompt or Project instructions.

Adapting by task type

Task typeWhat to emphasise in the prompt
AnalysisProvide the data; state the question and criteria; ask Claude to separate findings from interpretation and to show what each conclusion is based on.
ResearchDefine the question, scope (time period, region, sources), source quality, and ask for citations. Use web search or Research for current information.
DraftingAudience, purpose, tone, length, key points, structure; an example of the desired style.
BrainstormingAsk for many, varied options; defer judgement; then narrow down in a follow-up with explicit criteria.
🇺🇦 Простими словами

Хороший промпт = хто ти (роль) + що відбувається (контекст) + що зробити (задача) + обмеження + у якому вигляді (формат) + приклад. Велику задачу розбивай на кроки і перевіряй кожен крок. Якщо результат поганий — не тисни «перегенерувати», а скажи конкретно, що змінити.

Exam traps
  • “Tell Claude to be 100% accurate” or writing in capitals does not make output accurate. Grounding in sources and verification do.
  • One giant prompt for a multi-part deliverable is usually worse than decomposed steps with review points.
  • Regenerating without changing the input is not iteration.

Domain 2. Output Evaluation and Validation

21% of the exam · about 13 questions · the largest domain

Official objectives

  • Evaluate Claude-generated outputs for accuracy and completeness
  • Identify hallucinations, inconsistencies, and biases in responses
  • Apply fact-checking and validation techniques
  • Determine when human review or additional verification is required
  • Edit, adapt, refine, and compare outputs for the intended audience
  • Organise and curate information; choose output formats (artifacts, inline, structured data)

The evaluation checklist

DimensionQuestion to ask
AccuracyIs every claim true and supported by the source?
CompletenessDid it cover everything requested, and leave nothing important out of the source?
ConsistencyDo numbers, names, and dates agree across the whole output?
RelevanceDoes it answer the actual question for this audience?
Bias & fairnessDoes it stereotype, exclude, or present one side as the only view?
Fit for purposeRight tone, length, and format for where it will be used?

Hallucination red flags

  • Very specific details: exact percentages, subsection numbers, case names, page numbers, URLs, report titles.
  • Direct quotes attributed to real people.
  • Facts that don’t appear in the documents you provided.
  • Recent events beyond the training cutoff stated without a source.
  • Statements that agree suspiciously well with your own assumption (sycophancy).

Fact-checking and validation techniques

  • Verify against the authoritative source: the official regulation, the original contract, the primary data. This is the default correct answer for high-stakes claims.
  • Prioritise by risk: check numbers, names, dates, citations, quotes, legal or financial statements first.
  • Ask Claude to show its basis: “Quote the passage that supports each claim”, then check the quotes exist.
  • Ask Claude to list claims that need checking, then you do the checking.
  • Cross-check with independent sources; use web search or Research with citations for current facts, and open the cited pages.
  • Subject-matter-expert review for specialised content.
Key rule

Self-reported confidence is not a reliable accuracy signal. Asking Claude “are you sure?” or to rate its confidence, or regenerating and getting the same answer, does not verify anything. Verification means checking against a source outside the model’s own output.

When human review is required

Human review or expert verification is needed when output is high-stakes (legal, medical, financial, compliance, safety), affects people (hiring, performance, credit, eligibility), goes external (clients, regulators, the public, press), or contains factual claims someone will rely on. Low-risk internal work (brainstorm lists, first drafts, reformatting) needs lighter review. The person who sends it owns it.

Adapting and comparing outputs

  • Adapt for the audience: executives get impact and decisions first; technical teams get detail; customers get plain language.
  • When comparing two drafts, judge both against explicit criteria (accuracy, completeness, audience fit, requirements), not length or polish.
  • Editing for tone must never change the facts.

Choosing the output format

FormatUse when
Inline (in the chat)Short answers, quick explanations, conversational replies
ArtifactSubstantial, standalone content you’ll reuse, edit, iterate on, or share: documents, reports, code, diagrams, interactive tools. Shown in a separate window.
Structured dataTables, CSV, JSON, consistent fields, for spreadsheets, databases, or other tools
🇺🇦 Простими словами

Це найбільший розділ іспиту (21%). Головне правило: перевіряй конкретику перед тим, як відправити людям — цифри, дати, імена, цитати, номери статей і посилання. Перевіряти треба по першоджерелу, а не питати Claude «ти впевнений?». Якщо результат впливає на людей, гроші, закон чи здоров’я, або йде клієнту назовні — потрібна перевірка людиною чи експертом.

Артефакт — окреме вікно з документом/кодом, який можна редагувати і ділитися. Inline — коротка відповідь у чаті. Structured data — таблиця/CSV/JSON для інших інструментів.

Exam traps
  • A real-looking URL or report title is not proof the source exists or says that.
  • When two numbers conflict in Claude’s output, check the source; don’t pick the one that appears more often.
  • Rewording a summary to sound more formal does nothing for correctness.

Domain 3. Product and Model Selection

12% of the exam · about 7 questions

Official objectives

  • Select appropriate Claude product features (Projects, research mode, chat, artifacts)
  • Differentiate between Claude model types (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus)
  • Align model selection with task requirements (cost, speed, quality)
  • Understand and manage context limitations and memory (when to restart, summarise, or persist)

Features: which one fits?

FeatureWhat it isChoose it when

ProjectsSelf-contained workspaces with their own chat history, knowledge base (uploaded files), custom instructions, and memoryRecurring work that reuses the same context: a client, a campaign, a role, a team workflow
ResearchAgentic mode: Claude runs multiple searches that build on each other, across the web and connected sources, and returns a report with citations. Takes minutes, uses limits faster. Paid plans; needs web search on.Deep, multi-source questions needing current information and citations
Web searchClaude searches the web inside a normal replyQuick current facts
ArtifactsStandalone content in a side window: documents, code, diagrams, interactive apps; can be iterated and sharedSubstantial, reusable, editable outputs
Extended thinkingClaude reasons longer before answeringComplex analysis, multi-step reasoning, tricky problems
MemoryClaude remembers relevant context across chats; each Project has its own separate memory; you can view and edit itContinuity across conversations
Incognito chatA chat not saved to history or memorySelf-contained or exploratory conversations you don’t want remembered (data-retention rules still apply)
ConnectorsLinks to tools such as Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar (built on MCP)Claude needs live data from your tools instead of copied files
SkillsFolders of instructions, scripts and resources Claude loads when relevant to a taskRepeatable, specialised procedures (house style, document formats, team playbooks)

Models: Haiku, Sonnet, Opus

Model familyProfileTypical tasks
HaikuFastest, lowest costHigh-volume, straightforward tasks: short replies, classification, extraction, simple translation
SonnetBalance of intelligence, speed and costMost everyday business work: drafting, analysis, summarisation, coding
OpusMost capable of the three, slower and higher costComplex reasoning, nuanced analysis, demanding multi-step work where quality matters most

The rule from the official sample: match the model to the task requirements. Use a faster, lower-cost model for simple high-volume work; reserve the most capable model for complex reasoning. Anthropic’s current lineup also includes a newer top tier above Opus, but the exam guide v1.0 tests Haiku, Sonnet and Opus.

Context and memory management

  • Restart (new chat) when you switch topics, when a long conversation drifts, or when Claude starts missing earlier instructions.
  • Summarise before restarting: ask Claude to summarise key decisions, constraints and status, then paste that into the new chat.
  • Persist stable, reusable context in a Project (knowledge files and instructions) or memory, instead of re-pasting it every time.
  • What you say in one chat doesn’t automatically reach other chats in the same Project; durable context belongs in Project knowledge or instructions.
  • Each Project has its own memory, separate from other Projects. Incognito chats aren’t saved to memory or history.
🇺🇦 Простими словами

Haiku — швидко і дешево, для простих масових задач. Sonnet — «золота середина» для щоденної роботи. Opus — найрозумніший, для складного аналізу, але дорожчий і повільніший.

Project — коли задача повторюється і потрібен той самий контекст. Research — коли потрібне глибоке дослідження з актуальних джерел із посиланнями. Якщо чат став задовгим і Claude «забуває» — попроси підсумок і почни новий чат, а постійну інформацію збережи у Project.

Exam traps
  • “Always use the most capable model to maximise quality” wastes cost and speed on simple tasks.
  • Research is not a way to share a knowledge base with a team; a shared Project is.
  • The context window is not unlimited, and memory doesn’t mean Claude reads every past chat in every Project.

Domain 4. Workflow Integration and Solution Design

16% of the exam · about 10 questions

Official objectives

  • Apply Claude to analyse requirements and use cases
  • Use Claude for research, planning, and process optimisation
  • Use Claude to support solution design, development, and iteration
  • Integrate Claude into existing workflows to augment or redesign them
  • Communicate Claude’s value and limitations to stakeholders

A practical method

  1. Understand the requirement. What outcome does the business need? Who is affected? What does success look like?
  2. Map the current process step by step and find bottlenecks: repetitive, text-heavy, slow, or error-prone steps.
  3. Decide Claude’s role per step: automate, augment, or leave with a human. Mark the human checkpoints.
  4. Pilot on a small scope with real tasks.
  5. Measure against a baseline: time per task, quality or error rate, rework, satisfaction.
  6. Iterate, then scale: refine prompts, Projects and templates from feedback; document the workflow.

This is a sequence, so the order matters on the exam: understanding and mapping come before rollout.

Augment vs redesign

  • Augment: keep the process, make steps faster or better (Claude drafts, a human reviews and decides).
  • Redesign: rethink the process itself now that AI exists (e.g., merge steps, change who does what, add AI-assisted triage at the start).
  • Good candidates for Claude: repetitive, language-heavy tasks with clear quality criteria and room for human review. Poor candidates: decisions needing accountability or judgement Claude can’t provide, or tasks where errors are costly and hard to detect.

Using Claude across the work

  • Requirements analysis: give Claude meeting notes or briefs; ask it to extract requirements, flag ambiguities, conflicts, and open questions.
  • Research and planning: project plans, risk lists, stakeholder maps, competitive scans (verify facts).
  • Process optimisation: have Claude analyse process documentation and propose improvements, then validate with the people who do the work.
  • Solution design and iteration: templates, prototypes, workflows; test on real cases including edge cases; refine.

Communicating value and limitations

  • Use concrete evidence from pilots (time saved, quality metrics), not hype.
  • Be honest about limits: it can be wrong, needs current sources for recent facts, and outputs need review.
  • Explain where humans stay in the loop and who owns the result.
  • Set realistic expectations; never promise 100% accuracy.

Knowing when to escalate

The Associate role ends where technical build begins. Escalate to Claude Developers or Architects when the solution needs API integration, custom applications, agentic systems that act autonomously in business systems, embedding Claude in a product or website, or enterprise-scale architecture.

🇺🇦 Простими словами

Спочатку розберись у процесі (як він працює зараз і де «болить»), потім вирішуй, які кроки віддати Claude, а де залишити людину. Запусти пілот, виміряй результат у порівнянні з тим, як було, і лише потім масштабуй. Стейкхолдерам кажи чесно: ось що Claude дає, ось його обмеження, ось де людина перевіряє. Якщо потрібна інтеграція через API, чат-бот на сайт чи автономні агенти — це вже задача для розробників/архітекторів, ти ескалюєш.

Exam traps
  • “Roll out to the whole company immediately” before mapping, piloting, or measuring.
  • “Replace the team’s review step entirely” for high-stakes work.
  • Metrics like number of prompts written or output length don’t measure business value.

Domain 5. Configuration and Knowledge Management

12% of the exam · about 7 questions

Official objectives

  • Configure Claude Projects with instructions and knowledge sources
  • Manage uploaded knowledge and connectors (e.g., Google Drive, Gmail)
  • Create effective system-level instructions
  • Maintain and update Claude configurations, knowledge sources, and instructions

Projects: the two configuration layers

  • Project knowledge: files you upload (documents, text, code, data). Claude uses them as context in every chat in that Project.
  • Project instructions: standing guidance applied to every chat in the Project: role, tone, audience, format, rules.

Facts worth knowing: Projects are available on all plans; free users can create up to five. On paid plans, when project knowledge approaches the context limit, Claude automatically switches to RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), retrieving the relevant parts and expanding capacity (up to about 10×). On Team and Enterprise plans, Projects can be shared with view-only or edit permissions.

Knowledge management best practices

  • Use descriptive file names (“2026-Q4-Brand-Guidelines.pdf”, not “doc1.pdf”); Claude uses names to find the right information.
  • Keep one current version of each document; remove outdated versions so Claude doesn’t mix old and new facts.
  • Upload only what’s relevant to the Project; don’t mix unrelated work.
  • Never upload data your policy prohibits (see Domain 6).
  • Review knowledge on a schedule; assign an owner.

Writing effective system-level instructions

  • Say who Claude is in this Project, who the audience is, and what good output looks like.
  • Specify tone, format, length, terminology, and what to do when information is missing (“say it’s not in the sources”).
  • Point to the knowledge: “Use the price list in project knowledge for any pricing.”
  • Keep them durable and general; task-specific details belong in the individual chat. Overloaded instructions dilute focus.
  • Include an example of a good output if format matters.

Connectors

Connectors let Claude access tools such as Google Drive, Gmail and Google Calendar (and others via MCP, the Model Context Protocol), so it works with current data instead of uploaded copies. Good practice: connect only what the task needs (least privilege), follow your organisation’s approval (Team/Enterprise admins control which connectors are available), remember Claude sees what your account can see, and still verify what it retrieves.

Maintaining configurations

When a policy, price list, or process changes: update or replace the knowledge file, remove the old version, adjust instructions if needed, then test with a few typical questions to confirm the Project now answers correctly. Record what changed.

🇺🇦 Простими словами

Project має два «шари»: знання (файли) і інструкції (постійні правила поведінки). Файли називай зрозуміло і тримай тільки актуальні версії — старі видаляй. Інструкції пиши загальні й стабільні, а деталі конкретної задачі пиши в самому чаті. Конектори (Google Drive, Gmail) підключай лише ті, що потрібні, і згідно з політикою компанії. Коли щось змінилось — онови файл, прибери старий, протестуй.

Exam traps
  • Leaving old and new versions of a document side by side causes conflicting answers.
  • Putting one-off task details into Project instructions.
  • Connecting every account “just in case”.

Domain 6. Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use

15% of the exam · about 9 questions

Official objectives

  • Identify appropriate and inappropriate use cases
  • Apply data sensitivity, regulatory, and privacy considerations
  • Follow organisational AI policies and governance standards
  • Understand the ethical implications of AI usage

Appropriate vs inappropriate use

Generally appropriate (with review)Inappropriate or needs strong safeguards
Drafting emails, reports, documentationClaude as the sole decision-maker on hiring, firing, credit, medical or legal outcomes
Summarising and analysing approved documentsUploading data your policy prohibits
Brainstorming, planning, research with verificationFabricating testimonials, reviews, quotes, or data
First-pass screening that humans then reviewPublishing high-stakes content without human review
Internal knowledge assistants on approved contentDeceiving people about AI involvement where it matters

Data sensitivity and privacy

  • PII (personally identifiable information): names, emails, phone numbers, ID or account numbers, addresses.
  • Other sensitive categories: health data, financial account data, confidential client information, trade secrets, credentials.
  • Data minimisation: share only what the task needs.
  • Anonymise, pseudonymise, or redact identifiers before uploading when policy restricts them. This lets the task go ahead safely (official sample 3).
  • Use the approved workspace (e.g., the company’s Team/Enterprise account) for work data, not personal accounts, if policy says so.
  • Regulations such as GDPR or sector rules may apply; when in doubt, ask the data owner, legal, or the AI governance contact before uploading.
Key rule

An instruction to the model (“don’t retain this”, “forget this”) is not a data control. Controls are things like anonymisation, approved accounts, admin settings, and policy.

Organisational AI policy

Know and follow your organisation’s AI policy: approved tools and accounts, permitted data types, required reviews, disclosure rules, and who to ask. If the policy is unclear, ask before acting. Report incidents (e.g., data shared by mistake) through the proper channel.

Ethical implications

  • Bias and fairness: outputs can reflect biases in training data; review content that affects people.
  • Transparency: disclose AI use where expected by policy, clients, or context.
  • Accountability: the human and organisation using the output remain responsible.
  • Over-reliance: keep human judgement and expertise in the loop.
  • Intellectual property: respect copyright and confidentiality.

Відповідальність за результат завжди на людині.

Exam traps
  • “It’s only internal analysis, so upload as-is” ignores policy.
  • “Skip the task entirely” is usually wrong when anonymisation or an approved route makes it possible.
  • Asking the model to delete or not remember data is not compliance.

Domain 7. Troubleshooting and Optimization

10% of the exam · about 6 questions

Official objectives

  • Identify, diagnose, and resolve issues with underperforming prompts or poor outputs
  • Adjust approach based on feedback and results
  • Optimise workflows for efficiency and effectiveness

Diagnose the cause, then fix the input

SymptomLikely causeFix
Generic, bland outputMissing context, audience, purposeAdd context, audience, an example
Wrong formatFormat not stated, buried, or contradictedState the format explicitly; show an example; remove conflicting instructions
Invented factsNot grounded in sources; no permission to say “unknown”Restrict to provided documents; require quotes; allow “not in sources”
Outdated factsTraining cutoff, or stale Project filesUse web search/Research; update knowledge files
Forgets earlier instructionsVery long conversationSummarise and restart; move durable rules to Project instructions
Inconsistent results across a teamEveryone uses their own promptShared Project, template prompt, or Skill
Shallow reasoning on hard tasksModel or feature too light for the taskMore capable model, extended thinking, decomposition
Slow or costly at scaleOver-powered model for simple workFaster, lower-cost model; batch similar tasks

Working from feedback

  • Collect specific feedback from users and stakeholders (what was wrong, with examples).
  • Change one variable at a time and test on the same set of examples, so you know what worked.
  • If a change makes things worse, revert it.
  • Turn fixes into reusable assets: updated Project instructions, templates, Skills.

Optimising workflows

  • Stop re-pasting the same context: put it in a Project.
  • Use templates or Skills for recurring tasks.
  • Right-size the model for each step.
  • Decompose so that review effort lands where risk is highest.
  • Measure effectiveness (quality) as well as efficiency (time, cost).
🇺🇦 Простими словами

Поганий результат — це майже завжди проблема вхідних даних: бракує контексту, нечіткий формат, суперечливі інструкції, застарілий файл, не та модель. Спочатку знайди причину, потім змінюй одну річ за раз і перевіряй. Не тисни «regenerate» десять разів. Те, що спрацювало, збережи в інструкціях Project, шаблоні чи Skill.

Exam traps
  • Regenerating repeatedly without changing the prompt.
  • Changing many things at once, so you can’t tell what helped.
  • Blaming the model before checking for stale knowledge files or conflicting instructions.
